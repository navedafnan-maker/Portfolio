Shop operators in Kuakata

Rent, seasons and credit set shop earnings.

How it was done
Full title
Socio-economic and livelihood assessment of shop operators in Kuakata.
Question
How rent, seasonality and credit shape the earnings of general, dry fish and ethnic shops on the Kuakata coast, and how each depends on the ocean.
Context
Blue Economics, East West University. Prepared for Dr. A. K. Enamul Haque, Director General, BIDS.
Role
Group supervision, original draft of the report, visualisation, and shared data curation and formal analysis in a team of three.
Method
Face-to-face structured interviews on 27 and 28 March 2026 with 15 general, 12 dry fish and 8 ethnic shops, then descriptive statistics.
Policy
Low-interest credit for tourism-dependent micro-enterprises, storage for dry fish, links to certified fisheries, branding and packaging, and digital payments (only 20% of shops accept mobile banking).
Source
Kuakata field survey, 27 and 28 March 2026, n = 35. Reported shop income: Tk 35,000 to 50,000 a month in peak season (November to January) against Tk 10,000 to 20,000 in the off-season (April to September).

Solar home systems in Bangladesh

Cheap credit can pull households to diesel.

How it was done
Full title
Benefits to lighting: a cost-benefit analysis of solar home systems in Bangladesh.
Question
When does a rural household gain from switching kerosene lamps for a solar home system, and when does cheap credit push it towards diesel instead?
Context
East West University. Presented to Dr. A. K. Enamul Haque in a team of three. Based on the study in Bangladesh Priorities, Vol. 1.
Method
Reviewed and presented the cost-benefit model: 20-year panel life, battery replaced every 5 years, 15% down payment with 85% micro-credit, and a $25 subsidy per unit. Three switching cases, at 3%, 5% and 10% discount rates.
Finding
Solar pays for households at any discount rate up to about 15.7% if micro-credit is available. Diesel sharing has a far higher benefit-cost ratio, so unrestricted credit can pull households towards diesel.
Emissions
Diesel costs the world $216.68 per household at 3% and $49.01 at 5%.
Subsidy
Diesel to solar: each $1 of subsidy returns $8.71 at 3% and $1.97 at 5%. The study reports no global figure at 10%.
Source
Total social benefits and costs, present value in Tk, as reported in the study. Negative values in brackets.

Security in Dhaka South

Police and residents both name unemployment.

How it was done
Full title
Security profile of Dhaka South City Corporation.
Question
How residents of Dhaka South experience crime, what they think causes it, and where their view matches that of the police.
Context
Urban Economics, East West University. Submitted to Dr. Biva Arani Mallik, May 2025, in a team of four.
Role
Surveyor and report co-author.
Method
Online questionnaire shared with residents across DSCC areas, with 69 responses, plus a key-informant interview with an Additional Deputy Commissioner at Dhaka Metropolitan Police Headquarters.
Finding
Residents and the police both point to unemployment as a root cause. 77.9% of respondents see street children as at risk of being drawn into crime, and low trust in the police keeps crimes unreported.
Source
DSCC resident survey, May 2025, n = 69.

What residents think causes crime nearby.

The most cited cause. The police officer interviewed named winning public trust as the force’s biggest challenge.

Do you feel safe in your neighbourhood?

Just over half feel safe, which matches the police view that crime had fallen from the year before.

Is crime in your area improving or getting worse?

The largest group thinks crime is getting worse, even though the police report a fall.

Main type of crime reported, as shares of respondents.

Theft dominates. Most respondents also said crime happens after sunset.

Methodology and math

Good X is ready-made garments, good Y is agricultural and tech goods, and Y is the numeraire, so the price p is Px/Py.

Technology (CET frontier). Output is bounded by (X/a)ρ + (Y/b)ρ = 1 with ρ > 1. The sliders set a and b, the most of each good the economy can make. A larger ρ makes the frontier more curved, so shifting labour between sectors costs more. The slope is MRT = (b/a)ρ (X/Y)ρ−1.

Preferences (Cobb-Douglas). The community utility is U = Xα Y1−α, so an indifference curve is Y = (U / Xα)1/(1−α) and MRS = αY / ((1−α)X). The share α is spent on garments.

Autarky. Production equals consumption, so the economy picks the point on the frontier with the highest utility, where MRT = MRS = Px/Py. The code finds it by bisection.

Free trade. At a world price pw the economy produces where MRT = pw, earns income I = pwQX + QY, then consumes CX = αI / pw and CY = (1−α)I. Trade is balanced, pw(QX − CX) = CY − QY. The gain from trade is Utrade / Uautarky − 1, never negative for a small economy that takes the world price as given.

Assumptions: two goods, one country that is too small to move world prices, no tariffs or transport costs, full employment. The numbers are illustrative, not data.

Six questions, answered.

What do you research?

I study how people earn a living and what gets in the way. So far: coastal shop operators in Kuakata and their rent and seasonal income, the costs and benefits of solar home systems for rural households, safety in Dhaka South, and the barriers that keep low-income women out of formal jobs.

What have you found?

In Kuakata, 65% of 35 shop operators named high rent as a major challenge and income falls 50 to 70% in the off-season. For solar home systems, benefits are 1.67 times costs at a 5% discount rate. In Dhaka South, 60.9% of 69 residents blamed poor law enforcement and 59.4% unemployment.

How does it fit TRACE?

TRACE works on trade, market access and policy reform. My Kuakata study looked at a dry fish supply chain and its storage and quality problems, my solar project was a cost-benefit test of a subsidy, and all my projects rest on primary surveys. Those are the methods the Research and Policy Development team uses.

Which tools do you use?

Stata for statistical analysis, Excel for structuring data and descriptive statistics, and survey design to get good data in the first place. I present results with data visualisation, PowerPoint and Canva.

I am a Research Intern in the Research and Policy Development team at TRACE Consulting, a Dhaka-based research and advisory firm working on trade, economic policy and regulatory reform.

A Bachelor of Social Science in Economics at East West University, Dhaka, since June 2022. Coursework includes micro, macro, urban, blue and development economics, cost-benefit analysis and public finance.
